# Reflection

## Where Codex struggled
Codex didn't run into many problems, but I had to be clear about what I
wanted. When a prompt was specific, like telling it the column names contain
spaces or asking it to leave out neighborhoods with fewer than 20 sales, it
did what I asked. A vaguer prompt would have left it to make its own choices,
so I spelled out each step.

## What surprised me
I was surprised by how organized the information was. Each step built on the
last, and the output was easy to follow, which made it easy to check the
numbers along the way. I was also surprised by the results. Size was a strong
predictor of price and explained about half of the differences, but location
still mattered a lot. Some neighborhoods sold well above what their homes'
size would suggest, and others sold noticeably below. Two houses of the same
size can sell for very different amounts depending on where they are.

## How my prompts changed
My prompts became more specific as I went. The first one only asked Codex to
check the data and not create any files. Later prompts listed exactly what to
calculate, print, sort, and save. By the end I was asking it to double-check
its own work by recomputing the NAmes result a second way, and to only make
claims the table supports. That mattered because the analysis shows where
price gaps exist, not why they exist.
