## Summary of Findings
After removing a small number of unusually large houses, I used a linear regression to estimate how much of a home's sale price is explained by its above-ground living area. Living area turned out to be a strong predictor: larger homes consistently sold for more, and size alone accounted for a substantial share of the differences in price. To see how location fits in, I compared each house's actual sale price to the price its size predicted, then averaged those differences by neighborhood, leaving out neighborhoods with too few sales to give a reliable picture. Some neighborhoods sold well above what their homes' size would suggest, while others sold noticeably below it. In plain terms, size explains a lot of a home's price in Ames, but location still matters: two houses of the same size can sell for very different amounts depending on where they are. This analysis shows where those gaps exist, not why; factors like home age, quality, and lot size likely play a role.
